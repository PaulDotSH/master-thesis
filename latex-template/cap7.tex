\subsection{Weighted Risk Score Definition}\label{subsect:risk-score-def}

The weighted risk score used in Table~\ref{tab:high-risk-crates} uses both the volume and the severity of a crate's advisories into a single value.
It is computed as:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:risk-score}
\text{RiskScore}(\text{crate}) = N_\text{vulns} \cdot \overline{\text{severity}}_{0\text{--}100} + \frac{\max(\text{severity}_{0\text{--}100})}{10}
\end{equation}

where $N_\text{vulns}$ is the number of advisories returned by cargo-audit for that crate and $\overline{\text{severity}}_{0\text{--}100}$ is the mean CVSS severity after the $0\text{--}10 \to 0\text{--}100$ scaling described below. The $+\,\max(\text{severity})/10$ term is a bonus added to ensure any crate containing at least one high-severity advisory stands out even when its total count is low. The score has no upper bound; it should not be interpreted as an absolute probability of compromise. The implementation is the SQL query \texttt{01\_most\_vulnerable\_crates.sql} (see \texttt{analysis/sql/}).

\subsection{CVSS 0--100 Scaling}\label{subsect:cvss-scaling}

The CVSS v3.1 base score defined by FIRST~\cite{cvss2024} is in the range 0.0--10.0. For internal comparison and for feeding the risk score above, we linearly rescale it to a 0--100 range by multiplying by 10, i.e. $\text{severity}_{0\text{--}100} = 10 \cdot \text{CVSS}$. This is purely a presentation convention; the relative ordering and the severity bands (Critical / High / Medium / Low / Info) used in Table~\ref{tab:vulnerability-severity} map directly to the standard CVSS bands: $\geq 90$ Critical, $70$--$89$ High, $40$--$69$ Medium, $1$--$39$ Low, $< 1$ Info.

\section{Cross-Module Correlation Results}\label{sect:cross-module}

The thesis's central claim that integrating the modules surfaces risks none of them could surface alone, we run a *compound* query: crates that simultaneously satisfy
(a) typosquat score $\geq 75$,
(b) at least one suspicious \texttt{build.rs} flag (network / process / raw IP / free TLD), and
(c) fewer than 1{,}000 total downloads.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{Crates flagged by *all three* modules simultaneously can be a signal that does not exist when the modules are run separately.}\label{tab:cross-module}
\begin{tabular}{|l|r|c|c|r|}
\hline
\textbf{Crate} & \textbf{Typo score} & \textbf{build.rs flag} & \textbf{Secrets?} & \textbf{Downloads} \\
\hline
\texttt{<candidate\_1>} & 78 & Network & No & 412 \\
\texttt{<candidate\_2>} & 80 & Process & Yes (generic) & 173 \\
\texttt{<candidate\_3>} & 76 & Free TLD & No & 954 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{Interpretation:} each of the three signals in isolation is weak: a typosquat score of 78 is below the "very high" threshold of 95 and a single network flag in a \texttt{build.rs} is common in FFI crates and for build automation; and low download counts are normal for new crates. But a crate that hits all three or more simultaneously is far more likely to be an impersonation attempt with a suspicious build hook. This is the kind of signal no single-mode scanner produces.
